The water cycle moves water continuously around Earth. First, the Sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers. This heat turns the water into invisible vapor—a process called evaporation. As the vapor rises, it cools and changes back into tiny droplets, forming clouds; this is condensation. When the droplets grow heavy enough, they fall to the ground as rain, snow, sleet, or hail—collectively called precipitation. After reaching the land, water can flow downhill in streams and rivers back to the oceans, or soak into the soil where plants absorb it. The cycle then starts again.

Answer Key

  1. Evaporation, condensation, and precipitation – the three basic steps that move water around.
  2. Evaporation turns liquid water into water vapor (gas).
  3. Sunlight provides heat, increasing water molecules’ energy so more of them escape into the air.
  4. Condensation is the cooling of water vapor back into tiny liquid droplets.
  5. The droplets group together, becoming visible clouds when enough water vapor condenses.
  6. Precipitation is any form of water—rain, snow, sleet, or hail—that falls from clouds.
  7. When droplets grow heavy enough, they fall to the ground.
  8. Water can flow downhill in streams and rivers back to the oceans, or soak into the soil where plants absorb it.
  9. Plants absorb water from the soil, so the water cycle provides the water they need.
