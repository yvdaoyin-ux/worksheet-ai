The water cycle moves water continuously between Earth’s surface, the atmosphere, and underground. Solar energy causes water in oceans, lakes, and rivers to evaporate, turning it into water vapor. As the vapor rises, it cools and condenses into tiny droplets, forming clouds. When clouds become heavy, precipitation—rain, snow, sleet, or hail—falls back to the land. Some water soaks into the soil, becoming groundwater, while the rest runs over the surface as runoff, eventually returning to bodies of water. This cycle supplies fresh water for plants, animals, and people and helps regulate the planet’s temperature.
