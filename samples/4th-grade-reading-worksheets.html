Every Saturday morning, Maya heads to the Willow Creek Community Garden, a bright plot of raised beds surrounded by oak trees and a hummingbird feeder. She greets Mr. Alvarez, the garden coordinator, and puts on her straw hat before slipping her gloves on. First, Maya checks the lettuce rows, pulling out a few weeds that threaten to steal the soil’s nutrients. Next, she waters the young tomato seedlings, careful not to splash the soil too hard. Suddenly, dark clouds gather, and a light rain begins to patter on the leaves. Maya runs to the shed, grabs a tarp, and gently covers the beds to keep the seedlings dry. When the rain stops, she watches as tiny green shoots push through the earth, especially the carrot tops that peek shyly from the soil. Proud of her hard work, Maya records the day’s observations in her garden journal and promises to return next week, confident that the garden will keep growing. She also shares a few extra tomatoes with her neighbor, Mrs. Patel, who loves to make fresh salsa. Later, Maya stops by the garden’s little greenhouse, where she watches butterflies flutter around the blooming marigolds.
