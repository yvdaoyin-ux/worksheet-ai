The Life Cycle of a Butterfly

Butterflies start as tiny eggs on a leaf. When an egg hatches, a hungry caterpillar (also called a larva) comes out. The caterpillar eats leaves and grows bigger. After it is big enough, it makes a soft shell called a chrysalis. Inside the chrysalis, the caterpillar changes into a beautiful adult butterfly. The adult butterfly spreads its wings, drinks nectar from flowers, and later lays new eggs. This whole process—egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, butterfly—is called a life cycle. Each stage is important for the butterfly to grow and live.

Answer Key

  1. Egg – the very first stage.
  2. Caterpillar (larva) – the baby after the egg hatches.
  3. Leaves – they give the caterpillar energy to grow.
  4. Chrysalis stage – when the caterpillar makes a shell.
  5. Chrysalis – the soft shell that protects the change.
  6. Adult butterfly – this is when wings appear.
  7. Nectar – sweet liquid from flowers that gives energy.
  8. It eats a lot of leaves so it can grow big enough.
  9. It is a shell that lets the caterpillar change into an adult.
  10. Each stage leads to the next, so the butterfly can grow from egg to adult.
