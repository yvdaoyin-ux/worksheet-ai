The Water Cycle

The water cycle shows how water moves continuously around Earth. The sun’s heat makes water in oceans, lakes, and rivers turn into invisible vapor—this is evaporation. Warm air carries the vapor upward, where it cools and changes back into tiny droplets, forming clouds; this is condensation. When the droplets grow heavy, they fall as rain, snow, or sleet—this is precipitation. The water then collects in bodies of water or soaks into the ground, becoming groundwater. From there, the cycle begins again. The water cycle helps plants grow, supplies drinking water, and shapes weather patterns.

Answer Key

  1. Evaporation – the change from liquid water to water vapor.
  2. The sun’s heat warms the water, turning it into invisible vapor.
  3. Condensation – the change from water vapor back to liquid droplets.
  4. When the vapor rises, it cools and changes back into tiny droplets, forming clouds.
  5. Precipitation – water that falls from clouds as rain, snow, or sleet.
  6. When the droplets grow heavy, they fall as precipitation.
  7. It can collect in rivers, lakes, oceans, or soak into the ground as groundwater.
  8. Groundwater is water that has soaked into the ground and later becomes part of the water cycle again.
  9. The water cycle provides water that helps plants grow.
  10. The water cycle moves water around Earth, shaping weather patterns.
